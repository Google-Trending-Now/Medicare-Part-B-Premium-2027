Medicare Part B Premium 2027: New Monthly Costs, Deductible, and Income Brackets

Last Updated: November 18, 2026

The Medicare Part B premium 2027 is officially set at $209.40 per month for most Medicare beneficiaries. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) announced the new rate on November 17, 2026. This represents a $6.50 monthly increase compared with the 2026 standard premium of $202.90.

Along with the monthly premium increase, the annual Medicare Part B deductible will rise to $292 in 2027, compared with $283 in 2026.

These changes are important for older adults, people with disabilities, retirees, and anyone planning their healthcare expenses for the coming year.

In this guide, you will learn how much Medicare Part B costs in 2027, who pays more because of income, how the deductible works, and what beneficiaries can do to prepare for the new rates.

What Is the Medicare Part B Premium for 2027?

The standard Medicare Part B monthly premium for 2027 is $209.40.

Most people enrolled in Original Medicare pay this standard amount. However, beneficiaries with higher incomes may have to pay an additional charge known as the Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount (IRMAA).

Here is a quick overview of the official 2027 Medicare Part B costs:

Medicare Part B Cost 2027 Amount
Standard monthly premium $209.40
Annual deductible $292
Monthly premium increase $6.50
Previous standard premium (2026) $202.90
Annual standard premiums $2,512.80

The new premium takes effect on January 1, 2027.

Medicare Part B Income-Related Premiums for 2027

Not everyone pays the same Medicare Part B premium.

Medicare uses a system called the Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount (IRMAA). Under this system, people with higher incomes pay an additional amount on top of the standard monthly premium.

2027 Medicare Part B IRMAA Brackets

Individual Income Married Filing Jointly Monthly Premium
$112,000 or less $224,000 or less $209.40
$112,001–$141,000 $224,001–$282,000 $293.10
$141,001–$177,000 $282,001–$354,000 $418.80
$177,001–$212,000 $354,001–$424,000 $544.40
$212,001–$499,999 $424,001–$749,999 $670.00
$500,000 or more $750,000 or more $711.80

These income thresholds determine how much beneficiaries pay each month.

For example, an individual with an annual income of $150,000 falls into the $418.80 monthly premium category for 2027.

People with lower incomes generally pay only the standard monthly premium of $209.40.

What If You Are Married and File Taxes Separately?

Married beneficiaries who lived with their spouse during the tax year but file separate tax returns have different IRMAA rules.

  • Income of $112,000 or less: $209.40 per month.
  • Income above $112,000 but below $388,000: $670.00 per month.
  • Income of $388,000 or more: $711.80 per month.

How Does Medicare Calculate Your 2027 Part B Premium?

Medicare generally uses your tax information from two years earlier to determine whether you owe an income-related premium adjustment.

For 2027, this usually means Medicare will use your 2025 tax return.

Your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) helps determine which IRMAA income bracket applies to you.

  • If your 2025 income falls below the applicable threshold, you generally pay the standard premium.
  • If your income exceeds the threshold, you may pay an additional monthly amount.
  • If your income falls into a higher bracket, your total monthly premium increases.

Can You Appeal a Higher Medicare Part B Premium?

Yes. You may be able to request a reconsideration if your income has decreased because of certain life-changing events.

Qualifying events may include:

  • Retirement.
  • Marriage.
  • Divorce or annulment.
  • Death of a spouse.
  • Loss of income-producing property.
  • Reduction or loss of certain pension income.

If your financial situation has changed significantly since your 2025 tax return, contact Social Security to ask whether you qualify for an IRMAA reduction.

Visit the official Social Security website: SSA.gov.

What Does Medicare Part B Cover in 2027?

Medicare Part B is medical insurance that helps cover outpatient healthcare services.

It is different from Medicare Part A, which primarily covers inpatient hospital care.

Some common Medicare Part B services include:

  • Doctor visits.
  • Outpatient hospital services.
  • Preventive screenings.
  • Certain laboratory tests.
  • Ambulance services when medically necessary.
  • Durable medical equipment.
  • Certain home healthcare services.
  • Mental health services.
  • Some outpatient medical treatments.

After meeting the annual deductible, beneficiaries generally pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount for covered Part B services when the provider accepts assignment.

How Much Is the Medicare Part B Deductible in 2027?

The official Medicare Part B annual deductible for 2027 is $292.

This means beneficiaries generally must pay the first $292 of covered Part B expenses before Medicare begins paying its share under the applicable coverage rules.

  • 2026 deductible: $283.
  • 2027 deductible: $292.
  • Total increase: $9.

Once you meet the deductible, you generally pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount for covered services, unless another coverage or payment rule applies.

How Do You Pay Your Medicare Part B Premium?

Most Medicare beneficiaries who receive Social Security retirement benefits have their Part B premiums automatically deducted from their monthly Social Security payments.

If you are not receiving Social Security benefits, you may receive a Medicare premium bill.

Common payment methods include:

  1. Automatic deductions from Social Security benefits.
  2. Medicare Easy Pay.
  3. Online payments through your Medicare account.
  4. Other payment methods listed on your Medicare bill.

Visit Medicare.gov to review available payment options.

Can You Get Help Paying Medicare Part B Premiums?

Yes. People with limited income and resources may qualify for assistance through Medicare Savings Programs.

Depending on the program, assistance may include:

  • Payment of Medicare Part B premiums.
  • Help with Medicare deductibles.
  • Help with coinsurance.
  • Assistance with certain other Medicare expenses.

The Qualified Medicare Beneficiary (QMB) program is one example of a Medicare Savings Program.

Eligibility requirements vary by state and can change over time. Contact your state Medicaid office to find out whether you qualify.

When Does the New Medicare Part B Premium Start?

The new Medicare Part B premium becomes effective on January 1, 2027.

Beneficiaries should review their Social Security statements and Medicare notices to understand their updated monthly costs.

If you pay your premium directly, check your Medicare billing information for the applicable 2027 amount.
